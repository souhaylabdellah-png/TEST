Five reasons runners come back

Flat, cold, salty, and stupidly beautiful

Tangier sits at sea level on a strait 14 kilometres wide. Late October gives you 18 °C, an Atlantic headwind on one shoulder and a Mediterranean tailwind on the other, and a course that spends most of its distance within sight of water.

Two seas

Cap Spartel

At kilometre 32 the headland splits the water. Atlantic to your left, Mediterranean to your right, Spain on the skyline.

Sea level

Fast by design

Total elevation gain is about 210 m, and 150 m of it is one climb you finish before halfway.

Kilometre 18

The course · 42.195 km · single loop

Ten legs, one hill, two oceans

The route leaves the Grand Socco heading east along the bay, climbs into the Kasbah, then runs the Atlantic coast road out to the lighthouse and back. Numbered by kilometre marker, because that is the order your legs will meet them in.

  1. 0.0

    Grand Socco

    Gun at 08:00 under the medina walls. The first kilometre drops 20 m toward the port — everybody goes out too fast here. Everybody.

  2. 3.4

    Corniche

    Straight, wide, dead flat along the Bay of Tangier. Ferries stacking up offshore. This is where you settle into goal pace.

  3. 8.1

    Cap Malabata

    The eastern turnaround at the lighthouse. First look back at the city, first crosswind off the Mediterranean.

  4. 14.0

    The port

    Back past the ferry terminal. Ships sound their horns as the lead pack comes through — it is loud, and it is worth about ten seconds of free pace.

  5. 17.9

    Kasbah climb

    The one real hill: 150 m of gain over 2.1 km, the last 400 m on cobbles through Bab el Bahr. Walk it if you have to. Nobody is judging.

  6. 21.1

    Café Hafa · halfway

    Cliff terrace above the strait. On a clear morning you can see Tarifa. Split mats here — this is the number you will quote for years.

  7. 26.5

    Atlantic road

    Long, exposed, gently rolling, headwind more often than not. The lonely part of the race. Run it with someone.

  8. 32.0

    Cap Spartel

    The lighthouse. Two seas visibly meeting below you, Europe across the water. If you have anything left, spend it in the next 5 km.

  9. 35.4

    Caves of Hercules

    Turn for home along the cliffs. Net downhill from here to the finish, which is either a gift or a cruelty depending on your quads.

  10. 42.195

    Finish · Grand Socco

    Back up through the gate you started at, into the square, tea in hand within ninety seconds.

The question everyone asks

How do you run a marathon in under two hours?

Until April 2026 the honest answer was “nobody has, in a real race.” That answer expired. Here is what it actually took, what it would take you, and the version of “sub-2” that is genuinely within reach.

1:59:30 Sabastian Sawe · London · 26 April 2026 · world record, first legal sub-2

That is 2:50 per kilometre, or 4:33 per mile, or 21.2 km/h — held without a break for one hundred and nineteen minutes. Not one mile. All twenty-six of them, plus the bit at the end. Yomif Kejelcha ran 1:59:41 behind him on debut; Kelvin Kiptum’s 2:00:35 had stood as the record before that. Eliud Kipchoge’s 1:59:40 in Vienna in 2019 came first, but rotating pacers and a pace car made it an exhibition, not a record.

  • Engine A VO₂max around 85 ml/kg/min — roughly double a healthy untrained adult’s, and mostly decided by genetics before you start training.
  • Economy Exceptional running economy: less oxygen burned per kilometre than almost anyone alive, at about 55 kg carried on a light frame.
  • Volume 180–220 km a week, most of it easy, sustained for a decade. The record is built in the boring kilometres, not the fast ones.
  • Fuelling 90–120 g of carbohydrate per hour, drunk on the move, with a gut trained over years to absorb it at race pace.
  • Kit and course Carbon-plated super shoes (~4% economy), a flat sea-level loop, 8–12 °C, and a pack to draft — each worth seconds you cannot spare.
  • The truth Fewer than five people on the planet can do this today. It is not a training plan you can buy. That is what makes it worth watching.

There is a sub-2 that is actually yours

Run the half in under two hours. That is 5:41 per kilometre — a pace a committed runner can build to in one training block, and about half the field’s goal on race morning. Tangier’s half starts at 08:30 on the same course, and finishes in the same square, with the same tea.

Or take the full at 5:41/km and finish in 3:59:5x, which is the goal that ninety percent of first-time marathoners actually chase. Both of those are real. Neither of them needs a VO₂max of 85.
